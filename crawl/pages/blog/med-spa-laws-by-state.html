There’s never been a better time than the present to open a med spa. Valued at 18.1 billion in 2023, the industry is booming and growing quickly. However, opening a med spa isn’t an option for everyone. Med spa laws can be tricky to navigate and vary greatly between states.

The guide reveals how med spa laws differ by state, ways to stay on top of regulatory concerns, and the states where anyone can own a med spa.

Medical Spa Regulations

While laws vary from state to state, many have the same regulations:

  • All services must be performed by licensed professionals
  • A medical director should demonstrate the competence and education required to supervise licensed staff
  • An initial consultation must be done before services are provided, usually by a physician

Unlike traditional spas, med spas are classified as medical service providers and thus must protect patient data, follow HIPAA laws, and be Cures Act compliant. They also have to comply with Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) standards. Fortunately, the right EHR and PM software ensures you follow patient data regulations without any further action on your part.

Many states require a med spa to be owned by a licensed physician with an extensive background in aesthetic medicine. States where anyone can own a med spa include Arizona, Alaska, Florida, Louisiana, Maine, Michigan, Mississippi, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Ohio, South Carolina, Utah, Vermont, and Virginia.

Regulations around med spa ownership can change any time at either the state or federal level. Practices should conduct follow-up research to determine what policies they must follow, as well as work with a technology provider committed to staying up -to -date on regulation changes.  

Med Spa Laws By State

Running a med spa is a different beast depending on what state you live in. State laws often regulate who can own a med spa, advertising practices, taxation, and more. To help you determine if you meet your state’s regulations, we compiled information on each state’s med spa laws.

Alabama

  • Who can open and own a med spa?
    • Alabama requires med spa owners to be state-licensed physicians.
  • Advertising + marketing
    • Med spa marketing and advertising in Alabama must follow regulations to prohibit misleading or false claims about services. Practitioners’ qualifications and any risks must be included in advertisements.
  • Scope of practice
    • Med spas in Alabama can offer injectable neuromodulators, dermal fillers, laser hair removal, chemical peels, and IV therapy. Physicians and other appropriately licensed medical professionals (under the supervision of a medical doctor) can perform services.
  • Taxes on treatments
    • There are no taxes on med spa services in Alabama.
  • Liability and legal considerations
    • Med spas in Alabama must follow the legal and liability considerations that apply to other healthcare providers. This includes having a conformed consent procedure in place and carrying malpractice insurance.

Alaska

  • Who can open and own a med spa?
    • In Alaska, anyone can own a med spa, but licensed professionals must perform services.
  • Advertising + marketing
    • Med spa marketing and advertising in Alaska must follow regulations to prohibit misleading or false claims about services. Practitioners’ qualifications and any risks must be included in advertisements.
  • Scope of practice
    • Med spas in Alaska can offer injectable neuromodulators, dermal fillers, laser hair removal, chemical peels. Physicians and APRNs must perform services. An APRN must be supervised by a physician.
  • Taxes on treatments
    • There are no state taxes in Alaska, but some local governments apply taxes to med spa services.
  • Liability and legal considerations
    • Med spas in Alaska must follow the legal and liability considerations that apply to other healthcare providers. This includes having a conformed consent procedure in place and carrying malpractice insurance.

Recently Passed Med Spa Laws

Alongside state regulations, there are a few federal laws of note to med spa owners.

The Department of Justice also recently updated their regulations around sharing sensitive healthcare data with companies and individuals in other countries, providing another reason to opt for a HIPAA-compliant EHR/practice management provider.

Any med spa selling prescription medications, such as weight loss drugs, would benefit from researching the FDA’s new healthcare advertising regulations which emphasize stating any risks and contraindication information in a clear manner. Advertisements must also use consumer-friendly language and minimize distractions.

Previously, all businesses, including med spas, were required to report to the federal Beneficial Ownership Information database by the end of 2024, as per the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA). However, the U.S. Department of Treasury has since stated that due to a federal court order, companies are not required to submit this information and will not be subject to liability if they haven’t done so, so long as this court order stays in place.

Med spa owners also must be familiar with the Cures Act. Enacted in 2021, the Cures Act mandates all health technology systems must be able to transfer medical information to patients or other healthcare practices. Compliance with the Cures Act can be achieved by partnering with a technology vendor that adheres to all regulatory standards established by federal legislation.

Legislation on the Horizon

There are some proposed federal legislation changes that may impact med spas. The first is the Protecting Patients from Deceptive Drugs Ads Online Act, which was recently introduced in the senate. If passed, this bill may impact how companies advertise med spa services, most likely building off the updated guidelines released by the FDA. There are also proposed changes to HIPAA that were supposed to come into effect in 2025, though implementation is still pending.

The current administration has also indicated an interest in many reforms related to a broad swath of the healthcare industry that may impact med spas. Clinic owners are advised to be wary of new announcements regarding specific proposals.

“Med spa regulations are changing at an increasing rate,” said Robin Ntoh, Vice President of Aesthetics at Nextech. “It’s imperative for med spa clinicians and administrators to research these regulations and to consider how their technology resources are helping them stay up to date.”

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do Med Spa owners make?

How much Med Spa owners make varies, though the American Med Spa Association does have a few estimated stats based on revenue reporting from their members in 2022:

  • Single-location spas average $121,632 in monthly revenue
  • Overall, the average med spa revenue in 2022 was $1,982,896

Actual profit for the owners depends on how many employees there are, services provided, equipment costs, and more.

Are Med Spas required to be HIPAA compliant?

Since medical spas provide medical services, they have to be HIPAA compliant. HIPAA is a set of federal regulations to protect patient data. Fortunately, it’s easy to be HIPAA compliant if you choose a compliant EHR vendor.

What is the “FDA warning” on Med Spas?

A 2023 FDA warning cited concerns about the increased risks of scarring, skin infections, and serious complications from services provided by med spas. While this doesn’t change regulatory practices, it should impact how you market your med spa, including increased emphasis on the credibility of your services.
