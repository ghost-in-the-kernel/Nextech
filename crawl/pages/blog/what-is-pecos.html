Medicare enrollment is a foundational component of successful business for specialty healthcare practices in dermatology, ophthalmology, and plastic surgery. Before a single Medicare claim can be paid, providers and organizations must be enrolled through PECOS, the Provider Enrollment, Chain, and Ownership System.

For many practices, PECOS is one of the earliest and most consequential administrative hurdles they encounter, often before opening their doors or onboarding a new provider. Between credentialing requirements, identity verification, supporting documentation, and sometimes conflicting Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services instructions, the process can feel overwhelming. Even small mistakes or missed steps can delay approval and stall reimbursement, impacting cash flow at critical moments.

This guide breaks down what PECOS is, why it matters, and how specialty practices can navigate enrollment step by step. You’ll learn what to expect during the process, common pitfalls to avoid, timelines to plan for, and best practices to maintain compliance so your team can stay focused on patient care and practice growth instead of paperwork.  

What Is PECOS?

PECOS stands for Provider Enrollment, Chain, and Ownership System. It is the CMS-managed platform used to enroll healthcare providers and organizations in Medicare and to maintain accurate enrollment records over time.

PECOS is often referenced during credentialing and onboarding conversations, but many providers and staff aren’t fully clear on what it actually does or why it carries so much weight. Understanding PECOS at a high level helps practices approach Medicare enrollment with confidence and avoid costly missteps.

Why Navigating PECOS Is Important for Specialty Practices

PECOS directly affects how quickly you can see Medicare patients and get paid for the care you provide. In ophthalmology, dermatology, and plastic surgery, where reimbursement margins are tightening and staffing resources are limited, even minor enrollment delays can create meaningful disruptions to operations and revenue.

PECOS serves as the foundation of your Medicare participation, linking your credentials, practice details, and ownership information directly to reimbursement eligibility.  

Without accurate and up-to-date PECOS enrollment:

  • Medicare claims cannot be paid.
  • Credentialing timelines are extended.
  • Billing workflows become inconsistent.
  • Practices face higher audit and compliance risk.

PECOS also plays a critical role in protecting the Medicare system by validating provider identity and ownership, helping reduce fraud while ensuring legitimate practices are paid accurately and on time.

What Does PECOS Do?

At its core, PECOS exists to ensure that Medicare payments are made only to qualified, legitimate providers and organizations. For specialty practices, it functions as both:

  • An enrollment gateway for Medicare participation
  • An ongoing compliance record that must remain accurate as your practice evolves

For Medicare, PECOS serves as the central enrollment and verification system. It allows CMS and Medicare Administrative Contractors (MACs) to:

  • Confirm provider identity and credentials
  • Verify practice locations and ownership structure
  • Track enrollment status across multiple locations and entities
  • Monitor compliance through periodic revalidation

Simplifying the Terminology

PECOS often gets lumped together with other CMS systems, which can create confusion. A simple way to think about it:

  • National Provider Identifier (NPI) identifies who you are.
  • PECOS confirms whether Medicare recognizes you and allows you to bill.
  • MACs process your enrollment and claims once approved.

Core Functions of PECOS

  • New enrollment: For newly established practices or newly hired providers
  • Updates and changes: Adding practice locations, ownership changes, or reassignment of benefits
  • Revalidation: Required every 3-5 years to confirm ongoing eligibility
  • Application tracking: Monitor submission status and receive alerts or requests for additional information

For multi-provider or multi-location specialty practices, PECOS becomes an essential ongoing operational responsibility.

Who Uses PECOS?

PECOS isn’t just for administrators. It affects nearly every role involved in delivering and billing care. Physicians, advanced practice providers, and administrative staff all interact with PECOS directly or indirectly as part of Medicare participation.

Users typically include:

  • Physicians and specialists (ophthalmology, dermatology, plastic surgery)
  • Nurse practitioners and physician assistants
  • Practice administrators, office managers, and credentialing staff
  • Group practices and facility-based entities

Even when administrative staff handle enrollment, providers must still approve and validate key steps.

How to Enroll in PECOS: Step-by-Step Guide

PECOS enrollment follows a defined process, but the steps aren’t always intuitive, especially for new practices or first-time enrollees. Breaking the process into clear stages helps specialty practices avoid common errors and set realistic expectations for approval timelines.

Step 1: Create or Confirm Your NPI

Before accessing PECOS, every provider and organization must have a National Provider Identifier (NPI).

  • Individual providers need an NPI Type 1
  • Organizations need an NPI Type 2

Estimated timeframe: Same day to a few business days.

Tip: Ensure legal names and addresses exactly match state licensure and IRS records.

Step 2: Create an Identity & Access Management (I&A) Account

The CMS I&A system verifies user identity and controls access to PECOS.

  • Each provider must have their own I&A account
  • Organizations designate an Authorized Official (AO) or Delegated Official (DO)

Estimated timeframe: 1-3 days.

Tip: Use consistent email addresses and keep login credentials secure.

Step 3: Register for PECOS Access

Once the I&A account is active, users request access to PECOS through the CMS portal.

Estimated timeframe: 1-2 days.

Tip: Confirm user roles carefully. Incorrect permissions can stall submissions.

Step 4: Initiate the Medicare Enrollment Application

In PECOS, practices begin the formal Medicare enrollment application. This includes:

  • Provider or organizational details
  • Practice locations
  • Reassignment of benefits, if applicable

Estimated timeframe: 1-2 weeks to complete accurately.

Tip: Save progress frequently and review entries carefully before submission.

Step 5: Upload Supporting Documentation

Common documentation includes:

  • State licenses and certifications
  • Ownership and organizational structure
  • Practice addresses and contact details

Estimated timeframe: Varies based on document availability..

Tip: Keep digital copies organized and clearly labeled to avoid resubmission requests.

Step 6: Submit and Track the Application

After submission, PECOS allows real-time status tracking.

Estimated timeframe: Initial review typically begins within days.

Tip: Assign a team member to monitor status weekly.

Step 7: Respond to MAC Requests

Medicare Administrative Contractors may request clarification or corrections.

Estimated timeframe: Responses are typically due within 30 days.

Tip: Respond promptly. Delays reset processing timelines.

PECOS Updates Specialty Practices Should Know

CMS planned for an overhauled “PECOS 2.0” to launch in 20023/24, but shifting administrative priorities over modernization caused some changes to be delayed until 2025. CMS continues to refine PECOS to improve security, accuracy, and oversight. Staying informed about updates helps specialty practices plan onboarding and enrollment timelines more effectively.

What’s Changing in 2026

  • Increased digital identity verification requirements
  • Enhanced portal security and user permissions
  • Improved application status notifications

Why It Matters

These updates aim to reduce fraud and errors, but they can also introduce learning curves for staff. Practices onboarding new providers should plan extra time for enrollment and verification.

Opting In vs. Opting Out of Medicare

Deciding whether to participate in Medicare is a strategic business decision, not just a clinical one. Understanding how PECOS fits into opt-in and opt-out choices helps practices avoid unintended long-term consequences.

Why Most Practices Opt In

  • Access to Medicare reimbursement
  • Credentialing expectations from commercial payers
  • Broader patient population reach

When Opting Out May Make Sense

  • Fully cash-pay cosmetic practices
  • Practices with no intent to treat Medicare beneficiaries

Risks of Opting Out

  • Inability to bill Medicare for two years
  • Patient confusion and missed revenue opportunities
  • Limited flexibility if business strategy changes

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Many PECOS issues aren’t caused by complexity, but by small oversights and preventable errors that snowball into major delays.

Incomplete or Unsigned Documentation

Avoid it by: Using a checklist and verifying signatures before submission.

Ownership Information Mismatches

Avoid it by: Ensuring PECOS entries match state and IRS filings exactly.

Missed Revalidation Deadlines

Avoid it by: Tracking deadlines in a shared compliance calendar.

Failure to Update Locations or Services

Avoid it by: Updating PECOS promptly when adding locations or telehealth services.

What to Expect After You Submit a PECOS Application

After submission, practices often feel uncertain about next steps and timelines. Understanding what happens behind the scenes helps set realistic expectations and reduces anxiety during the waiting period.

Typical Timeline

  • Review and approval often take 60-90 days
  • Complex applications may take longer

Communications from MACs

  • Requests for clarification
  • Approval notices
  • “Returned for Corrections” alerts

When Reimbursement Begins

  • In some cases, Medicare allows retroactive billing, but approval must occur first

Monitoring and Maintaining PECOS Compliance

Enrollment approval is just the beginning of PECOS compliance. It also requires ongoing attention as providers, locations, and services change. Establishing clear internal ownership helps practices stay compliant without constant firefighting.

Best Practices

  • Regularly confirm enrollment status
  • Track revalidation cycles
  • Assign clear internal ownership for compliance
  • Multi-location and multi-provider practices benefit from centralized oversight

Frequently Asked Questions

Even experienced practices often have lingering questions about how PECOS applies in specific scenarios. Addressing common concerns – such as locum providers, retroactive billing, and telehealth – helps teams avoid assumptions that could impact reimbursement.
