Obstacles to premium revenue growth

Whether patients are considering LASIK, cataract surgery, RLE, dry eye treatment, glaucoma care, or oculoplastics procedures, practices face the same challenge: creating an integrated path from inquiry through treatment to ensure patients understand all their options and receive the exact care they need.

Challenge
Patients may complete a LASIK inquiry form, but LASIK may not always be the best fit for their vision goals—and they may be contacting other practices at the same time.

Opportunity
Use timely follow-up, education, and easy consult scheduling to help interested patients understand their options and move confidently to the next step.

Challenge
Patients considering LASIK may be a better fit for RLE surgery ... so how can we identify and educate these patients? And can we find prior LASIK consults that might be ideal candidates for RLE?

Opportunity
Create visibility into patient interests and connect patients with the right treatment pathway through automated campaigns and data integrations between CRM and PM systems.

Challenge

Opportunity
Support premium lens and surgery discussions with timely communication and patient engagement.

Oculoplastics volume can fluctuate, even when there are opportunities to educate and engage more patients around cosmetic and functional procedures.

Create a seamless patient journey from inquiry, education, and scheduling through financing and billing to help patients see and feel their best.

Keep up with the evolving patient journey

See how CRM helps practices connect every interaction, personalize engagement, and support patients throughout their journey.

One of the interesting parts of ophthalmology today is that one inquiry can actually lead to many possible, treatment options because the patient journey has transitioned from something very linear to something more dynamic. Let me give you an example. A person submits a LASIK form on your website. Your team calls them and books them for a LASIK consult. They schedule LASIK surgery. They receive LASIK surgery, and they're very, very happy. That's a great linear journey, and it does happen. But what if that patient isn't a candidate for LASIK surgery? What if they're a better candidate for another surgery you offer, like PRK or SMILE, or they'd be eligible for RLE or an ICL? Or what if they need dry eye treatment before surgery? Also, what if they're actually pre cataract? And you should have a whole different set of conversations with that patient. All those things are more of a dynamic patient journey, and they require a different approach to your technologies. Not only that, but what if after LASIK, they're actually eligible for additional procedures, or you want to keep them in your education sequence so you can keep treating them down the line. What are you gonna do with that journey to make sure that you are able to offer them everything they need for their vision? So one inquiry does not equal one treatment. One inquiry should be whatever number of treatments you need and open up the whole world, of options for your patients. Generating patient interest is only the very first step. What happens after the inquiry is just as important. The patient needs to learn more about that practice. Are they gonna learn about it because you're open from nine to five? Are they gonna learn about it during your hours by picking up the phone and calling you? No. Patients need to be met where they find it convenient. At 11:00 at night when they're browsing the Internet and they wanna find out more information about you, they need to find it on your website. They need to find it in your social media. They also need to find it based upon information you're feeding back to them that is about your practice, that crafts the conversation, that crafts the information that you need them to know about you, that qualifies you as a practice. A lot of times when we look at the patient experience, we think about it as a journey. Where does that patient journey start and where does it end? How do we coordinate that journey so that the patient feels like it's connected? How do we make the patient understand that they're truly the sole focus of what we're trying to do for them. And so a lot of times when we look at that journey, we think of that journey as starting before they ever walk in the door. That journey starts with that initial inquiry, how they actually found you, how they actually learn about you, how they actually qualify that you're the right practice that they wanna see. And CRM does a great job of helping us do all of that at the convenience of the patient. So maybe that information is made available to them at 10:00 at night, or maybe it's part of the conversation that they receive when they pick up the phone and they engage with your practice. The point is is that we provide different ways for that patient to actually engage with you no matter where they are in their journey, whether it's at the inquiry, whether it's when they walk in the door, or even after they leave the practice. For the patient, they love knowing that everything is at their fingertips. It's convenient, and it's available to them whenever they need it or they want it. That's how we really connect everything to get the growth and the seamless behavior that we're looking for. Looks like a conversion. Looks like a happy patient. If you really consider all the friction that you can actually see within a business, a patient that actually can walk in the door and feel like every person knows everything they need to know about them, and they don't have to feel like that pay... They have to restate, well, this is what I just talked to that other person about. I just told another person. I just reiterated that answer or answered that question to the other person at the front desk. You don't want their head buried in a computer. You want them looking at the staff. You want them greeting your patients. You want them to actually talk to them and smile as they walk in the door. When you connect that patient journey, you free up your staff to feel like they can actually be focused on the patient.
